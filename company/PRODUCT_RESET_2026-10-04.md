# BORN WEIRD — PRODUCT RESET HANDOFF (Founder, 2026-10-04)

Source: Founder message to CEO-001, 2026-10-04. Stored verbatim in substance; it overrides v0.5 product direction where they conflict (see DECISION #017 and Genesis §74).

## Status
This document overrides the current product direction of v0.5 where it conflicts with the instructions below. It is not a request to incrementally improve v0.5. This is a product reset. The existing implementation, research, code, content and mechanics are assets and evidence, not constraints. Do not destroy the current version. Preserve it as a reference/archive before changing production.

## 1. Why we are resetting
Born Weird started from a simple intent: create a tiny, weird, fun experience that makes a human curious about themselves, produces an unexpectedly recognizable result, and makes them want to send it to another human.

During rapid iteration the product accumulated layers: birth date; life-in-weeks; numerology; astrology; Schwartz values; best–worst scaling; three game rounds; adaptive shadow questions; scoring; confidence; contradictions; archetypes; purpose; rarity; seven-day quests; AI handoffs; value passports; referral keys; analytics; future Intent OS concepts.

Together they changed the nature of the product. It is becoming **an assessment engine wrapped in a game**. That is NOT what we want to optimize. We want to rediscover **a tiny internet toy that happens to reveal something interesting about you.**

## 2. Founder intent
Create an extremely simple, weird and enjoyable internet game that a person can start almost immediately and finish in approximately 30–90 seconds. The player makes a small number of instinctive choices. The game then reveals something about the player that feels surprising, recognizable, slightly provocative and socially interesting. The result should become an identity object, "my Weird", that the player may naturally want to show another person. Seeing another person's Weird should create: "What would mine be?"

Desired reaction: **WTF. That's weirdly me. Try this.**

The product does NOT need to explain its intelligence. Complexity may exist behind the interface. It must not be experienced by the player.

## 3. Product principle
Optimize: FUN → CHOICE → CURIOSITY → SURPRISE → REVEAL → IDENTITY → SHARE → NEW PLAYER.
Not: ASSESSMENT → ANALYSIS → EXPLANATION → UTILITY. Utility may emerge later; virality and curiosity must first exist.

## 4. The magic trick
Before implementing anything, answer: what is the one magic trick of Born Weird? Candidate: "You make several fast choices and Born Weird reveals a contradiction about you that feels unexpectedly true." A hypothesis, not locked. It must be explainable in one sentence.

## 5. Do not optimize for psychometric completeness
Born Weird is a game, not a validated instrument. Do not sacrifice speed, fun, curiosity, surprise, emotional recognition or shareability for a more complete value model. We are testing whether people want the experience, not whether 9 questions infer an 8-dimensional hierarchy.

## 6. Birth date — change of status
Do not remove it permanently; do not assume it belongs in the core loop. It is an experimental variable: instant personalization, mystery, a natural "seed" — but also friction, astrology expectations, complexity, privacy, logic, risk of feeling like an astrology test. Birth date is a hook/seed candidate, not the truth engine. Test at minimum: **A — Birth first** (birth → game → reveal), **B — No birth** (game → reveal), optionally **C — Birth after** (game → "want to see what your birth seed says?" → enhanced reveal). Resolve through prototypes and humans, not internal debate.

## 7. Radical simplification
v0.5 is not the minimum feature set. Candidate target: 0–1 input before play; ~5–10 choices; one action per choice; ~30–60 s active play; one primary reveal; one identity artifact; one primary share action. Principle: remove until removing more destroys the magic trick.

## 8. Question design
INSTINCT → PRICE → SHADOW, four options per screen, best+worst, visible scoring, eight bars, "next" after every answer, reactions after every choice — none are sacred. Explore: binary instinct (BE FREE vs BE NEEDED), forced dilemma (WIN vs BE LIKED), swipe (← THIS / THAT →), progressive contradiction (later dilemmas challenge the emerging pattern). The player should react before they start intellectually modelling the questionnaire. If a question feels like homework, rewrite or remove it.

## 9. Result reset
The primary result should fit roughly on one mobile screen. Illustration (not required copy):
```
YOUR WEIRD
CARE > FREEDOM > IMPACT > STABILITY
YOUR CONTRADICTION
IMPACT × CARE
You want to matter. But not at the expense of people.
THE HUMAN BUILDER
[ SHARE MY WEIRD ]
```
One memorable result beats thirteen informative sections.

## 10. Result as identity object
Not a report: "This is my Weird." The share artifact should create curiosity without explanation; the recipient should ask "What would mine be?"

## 11. Temporarily freeze (not delete)
life-in-weeks; fresh-start milestone; Erikson stage; population-at-birth facts; "how calculated" explanations; visible eight-value HUD; purpose hypothesis; confidence section; fragile value; hidden value; rarity (unless it clearly improves identity/share); seven-day quest; value passport; JSON profile; Intent OS; runtime AI; three AI deep-links; compare-with-friend; Collide Timelines; community; DAO; tokens; monetization; elaborate referral genealogy.

## 12. Keep / protect
Brand BORN WEIRD. Tone: strange, playful, slightly provocative. Retro / weird digital-artifact visual direction if it helps (do not preserve components merely because they exist). Instant choices (reveal through decisions, not personality questions). Recognition ("that's actually me"). Contradiction (strong hypothesis, not dogma). Artifact (a visually recognizable share object). Privacy (minimal data). Technical simplicity (static, no account, no backend).

## 13. Agent organization reset
Founder role: INTENT OWNER / TASTE / CAPITAL — define desired effect; experience prototypes; give subjective taste feedback ("feels like homework", "made me curious", "I started thinking instead of playing", "generic", "I want to send this one"); rare intent decisions; approve exceptional resource/risk. Founder should NOT normally decide number of questions, scoring, wording, layout, architecture, behavioral model, or whether a screen survives. Agents own those.

## 14. Required agent functions (no agent theater)
- **PRODUCT / CEO** — owns experiment portfolio; does not defend existing implementation. "What is the smallest experiment that reduces the biggest uncertainty?"
- **GAME DESIGN** — "Is this actually fun to play?" Loop, pacing, curiosity, escalation, surprise, removing friction.
- **BEHAVIOR / RESEARCH** — "Does the reveal have enough basis in player behavior to avoid feeling arbitrary?" Model, interpretation, epistemic honesty, minimum signal. No veto merely because a simpler game yields less psychometric information.
- **GROWTH / VIRALITY** — "Why would one human send this to another human?" Artifact, curiosity gap, share copy, referral loop, friend-entry.
- **BUILDER** — builds selected prototypes; does not set direction.
- **QA / EVALUATION** — independent from Builder.

## 15–16. Prototype tournament
Do not build v0.6. Generate ≥5 substantially different versions differing in mechanics, not copy (e.g. A birth + 7 binary dilemmas + contradiction; B no birth + rapid swipe + identity; C birth only as mysterious seed + 5 choices; D progressive adaptive dilemmas that attack earlier choices; E a playful/absurd mechanic from Game Design). At least one must challenge current assumptions significantly. Each must be cheap enough that abandoning it is painless: rough, minimal polish, reuse infrastructure, hours not days, no production architecture.

## 17. Internal agent review
Each prototype: GAME DESIGN (fun, pacing, load), BEHAVIOR (signal, misleading claims), GROWTH (share impulse, recipient curiosity), QA (understandable without explanation). CEO selects ~2–3 finalists — not by average score; preserve diversity of hypotheses.

## 18. Human testing
Founder is one subject, not the market. Don't ask "do you like it?". Observe: started immediately? hesitated? confused? finished? emotional reaction? recognized themselves? screenshot/share? named someone to send it to? Follow-ups: Where did you want to quit? Where did you have to think too much? What surprised you? Generic or specific? Who would you send this to?

## 19. Initial success signals
START, COMPLETION, RECOGNITION, SURPRISE, SHARE INTENT, ACTUAL SHARE, REFERRED START. Don't overfit thresholds to tiny samples. Loop: PLAYER → RESULT → SHARE → NEW PLAYER.

## 20. North star for this phase
**DOES THE TOY REPRODUCE?** Not Intent OS, monetization, depth, scientific completeness, or autonomous-company sophistication.

## 21. Autonomous company experiment
Humans own intent, agents own execution. OBSERVE → HYPOTHESIS → PROTOTYPE → TEST → MEASURE → LEARN → ITERATE. Founder intervenes only on ambiguous intent, strategic forks, capital/risk thresholds, or when subjective taste is specifically valuable. Repeated implementation questions to the Founder = organizational failure.

## 22. Update Genesis
1 preserve v0.5; 2 update Genesis to the post-boot state; 3 incorporate this reset; 4 update agent responsibilities; 5 remove stale roadmap assumptions; 6 freeze nonessential work; 7 rewrite WORK_QUEUE around the tournament; 8 define minimum analytics; 9 no runtime AI; 10 no question-pool expansion; 11 no features added to v0.5. The next unit of progress is "we learned which tiny Born Weird people actually want to play".

## 23. First autonomous mission — FIND THE SMALLEST BORN WEIRD THAT FEELS ALIVE
1 analyze v0.5 only as evidence; 2 produce ≥5 radically simplified concepts; 3 evaluate independently; 4 select 2–3 finalists; 5 build lightweight playable prototypes; 6 internal QA; 7 prepare for human testing; 8 involve the Founder only when prototypes are ready to EXPERIENCE: "Here are three playable versions. Play each one. Do not analyze them while playing. Tell us which one feels alive."

## 24. Do not overcorrect
Values, birth date, Schwartz, quests, Intent OS, astrology/numerology, runtime AI, v0.5 — not wrong, not wasted. They are layers. First find the toy.

## 25. Final operating question
For every feature, screen, model or agent: does this make the magic trick stronger? NO → remove/freeze. UNKNOWN → test the cheapest version. YES → keep in the smallest form. And: can we learn the same thing without building this?

**End state:** the smallest Born Weird that causes: *I tried it. That was weird. That's kind of me. You need to try this.*
